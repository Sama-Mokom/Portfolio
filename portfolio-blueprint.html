1.5.4 GoldStrat / SMC_EA — algorithmic trading systems

PurposeMetaTrader 5 Expert Advisors implementing Smart Money Concepts / ICT-style strategies on XAUUSD, developed and refined through systematic backtesting.
OriginInitial files came from a friend who trades a funded account; the work since has been iterative improvement and correctness engineering.
Engineering problems solvedA position-sizing bug producing lot sizes roughly ten times too large, traced to a manual tick-value formula and fixed by delegating to the broker's own OrderCalcProfit() engine. A reward-to-risk imbalance caused by a target-finding function with no minimum-distance check. Order rejections caused by failing to query SYMBOL_TRADE_STOPS_LEVEL and failing to normalise prices to the tick grid.
Analytical findingsAn eight-year backtest showed net profit concentrated in the final two years of a gold bull run, with the preceding six years losing — identifying regime dependency rather than edge. Timeframe-alignment filtering between H4 bias and M15 entries produced no change in results, leading to the conclusion that state-based agreement between timeframes is not independent confirmation and that event-based triggers are required instead. Short trades underperform longs (21–35% versus 53–58% win rate); an event-based CHoCH confirmation is the proposed response.
DemonstratesThis is the strongest evidence in the entire record of scientific scepticism applied to one's own work. The finding "my strategy was riding a regime, not demonstrating an edge" is the single most impressive intellectual moment on file, and it is currently invisible to anyone reading the portfolio.
CaveatEstablished — self-described beginner-level, non-professional trader with no live trading experience. The portfolio must present this as quantitative systems engineering, never as trading expertise. Any framing that implies financial performance or invites others to trade must be avoided outright.

2. Portfolio Goals and Audience Analysis

2.1 What the portfolio must achieve

The brief is broad, so the goals must be ordered. In priority sequence:

  1. Convert a recruiter's thirty-second scan into an interview. The author has stated that missing portfolio and GitHub URLs are among his top career blockers. Everything else on this list is subordinate to this.
  2. Give a senior engineer something to respect. Not a technology list — evidence of judgement. The TOCTOU fix, the rejected reopen designs, the regime-dependency finding.
  3. Show the person, not just the practitioner. The explicit central ask of the brief.
  4. Make growth visible. An early-career engineer cannot compete on years of experience, but can compete on demonstrable trajectory.
  5. Establish a writing habit with somewhere to live. The blog is not decoration; it is the mechanism by which the portfolio stays alive between projects.
  6. Be the best artefact in the portfolio. A site that is fast, accessible and beautifully typeset is itself the strongest frontend evidence on offer.

2.3 Design tensions to resolve deliberately

  • Depth versus scannability. Resolved by progressive disclosure: every page and every case study is readable at three depths — headline, summary, full detail.
  • Education status versus professional ambition. Resolved by leading with artefacts and reasoning rather than titles. Keep education in the résumé rather than using it as the site-wide identity.
  • Personality versus professionalism. Resolved by concentrating personality in specific, bounded places (About, Now, blog voice, footer) rather than sprinkling it thinly everywhere.
  • Trading work versus conservative employer perception. Resolved by framing it strictly as quantitative systems engineering, foregrounding the falsification finding rather than any performance claim.

2.4 Success criteria

  • A recruiter can answer "level, stack, location, availability, contact" within thirty seconds without scrolling past the first screen and a half.
  • At least three case studies contain a genuine engineering decision with a named alternative that was rejected and the reason for rejecting it.
  • Lighthouse accessibility 100; full keyboard operability; WCAG 2.2 AA verified.
  • Core Web Vitals within the good band on a mid-range Android device over a throttled connection, not merely on a laptop.
  • No page requires JavaScript to convey its content.
  • A visitor can describe one non-technical thing about the author after reading the About page.

3.2.1 Notes on specific decisions

  • Experience, Education and Skills are not separate routes. They are sections within /about, deep-linkable by anchor (/about#experience) so that a CV or an email can point straight at them. Three separate thin pages would fragment one continuous story.
  • /lab exists alongside the hackathon and case-study material. 3.3 Global navigation strategy

  • A single persistent header: wordmark at left; Work, Writing, Lab, About centre or right; Contact as the one visually distinct action; a theme toggle at the far right. Five items is within comfortable scanning range; /now and /resume are reached from About, Home and the footer rather than crowding the header.
  • The header does not hide on scroll. Hide-on-scroll headers are a fashion that trades a persistent orientation aid for a few reclaimed pixels; on a content site that trade is not worth making. The header may reduce in height and gain a hairline bottom rule once the page has scrolled — a state change that communicates position rather than decorating.
  • The current section is marked with a persistent rule beneath the active item, not colour alone. Colour alone fails for colour-blind users and is disallowed by the contrast strategy in Deliverable 11.
  • A "skip to content" link is the first focusable element on every page.
  • The footer is a genuine secondary navigation surface: a short statement of what the site is, full route list including /now, /resume and /rss.xml, external links (GitHub, LinkedIn, email), location and timezone, a last-updated stamp, and a one-line colophon naming the stack. The colophon is not vanity — for this audience it is evidence.
  • Breadcrumbs appear on case studies and blog posts only, where hierarchical position genuinely needs stating.
  • On mobile, the header collapses to wordmark, theme toggle and a menu button opening a full-screen panel with generously spaced targets — not a cramped dropdown.

4. Portfolio Storytelling Strategy

4.1 The narrative thesis

One sentence the entire site exists to support:

A Software Engineer who builds systems for the conditions he actually lives in, and who learns on purpose rather than by accident.

Every page is answerable to that sentence. Content that does not support it, extend it or complicate it interestingly should be cut.

4.2 The time ladder

At five seconds

The visitor should know: who he is, what he does, where he is, and that this site was made with care. Delivered by a single typographic statement at the top of Home — name, discipline, location, current status — and by the quality of the type and spacing, which communicates care before a single word is read. No hero image, no animated headline, no scroll-to-reveal. The statement is present immediately and is selectable text, not an image.

At thirty seconds

The visitor should know: the kind of engineering he does, three concrete things he has built, and how to reach him or read further. Delivered by three selected work entries directly beneath the opening statement, each with a real one-line outcome rather than a technology list, plus a visible link to the CV. A recruiter who leaves at this point should leave with enough to decide.

At three minutes

The visitor should understand: how he thinks. Delivered by one case study read at depth — problem, constraint, the decision, the alternative rejected, the thing that went wrong, what changed as a result. This is where CampusDesk's two discarded reopen designs and GoldStrat's regime-dependency finding do their work. It is the difference between "he can use Laravel" and "he can be trusted with a subsystem."

At ten minutes and beyond

The visitor should understand: the trajectory and the person. Delivered by the About timeline, the Lab, the Now page, and the blog voice. This is where the non-technical material from Section 1.14 must land. Without it, the site plateaus at three minutes.

4.3 How personality is allowed to emerge

Personality is placed, not scattered. Sprinkling jokes through a technical site reads as nervousness; concentrating voice in defined places reads as confidence.

  • About, first person, full voice. Written the way he actually speaks, including the parts that are not flattering — resigning from Njomi when the load became unsustainable, choosing the slow path on CampusDesk, discovering that eight years of backtest were really two.
  • The "lessons learned" section of every case study. The only mandatory section in the framework, because it is where judgement shows.
  • The Now page. Short, dated, plainly written, allowed to include non-technical items.
  • Blog voice. First person, specific, willing to document the failure as well as the fix.
  • The footer colophon and the 404 page. Small, dry, human. Two places where a light touch costs nothing.

Deliberately excluded: animated avatars, "fun facts" grids, typewriter effects, emoji section headers, and anything that performs personality rather than expressing it.

4.4 Serving technical depth without excluding anyone

Three-layer disclosure, applied consistently:

LayerFormRule
Layer 1Plain-language summaryEvery page and case study opens with two or three sentences a non-engineer can read without stumbling. No unexplained acronyms. Always visible.
Layer 2Structured narrativeThe main body — problem, approach, decisions, outcome. Technical but explained. Always visible; never hidden behind an interaction.
Layer 3Deep technical detailCode, schema fragments, trace output, benchmark tables. Presented in explicitly labelled, collapsed disclosure elements — "Show the locking implementation", "Show the backtest breakdown". Uses native <details> so it works without JavaScript and is announced correctly by screen readers.

The critical rule: layer 3 is always collapsed by default and layers 1 and 2 are never collapsed. Hiding the narrative behind interaction is the most common failure mode of ambitious portfolios.

4.5 Recruiter fast path

A deliberate, designed path rather than an accident of layout: Home opening statement → three work entries with outcomes → prominent CV link → contact in the footer of every page. The CV page carries a plain "Download PDF" control with file size stated. Nothing on this path requires scrolling through a personal essay to reach.

4.6 Engineer deep path

Work index → case study → expandable technical layers → linked repository → related blog post → next case study. The "next case study" link at the foot of each study is what makes this path continuous instead of a series of dead ends.

4.7 Turning projects into case studies rather than screenshots

The single structural change that does this: every case study must contain at least one thing that did not work. A failure, a rejected design, a wrong assumption, a bug that took a day. Screenshots with technology badges are indistinguishable from one another; the story of a TOCTOU bug that survived its first fix is not. The framework in Deliverable 6 enforces this as a required field.

4.8 Suggested narrative progression

  1. Who — Home opening statement. Identity, discipline, place.
  2. Proof — three selected works, each with an outcome, not a stack list.
  3. Depth — a case study read in full.
  4. Trajectory — the About timeline: foundations, breadth, production contact, deliberate depth.
  5. Thinking — the blog.
  6. Present tense — Now and Lab. The site is not a monument; it is in progress.
  7. Person — interests, values, the nonprofit, the things outside the work.
  8. Invitation — contact, with a specific statement of what he is looking for rather than a generic "get in touch".

7. Design Research Findings

This section separates four categories that are usually conflated: durable principles, current trends, this document's recommendations, and matters of taste. Confusing them is precisely how portfolios end up looking like the thing this brief rejects.

7.1 Established principles

These are long-standing, widely evidenced, and not matters of opinion.

  • Measure governs readability. Sustained reading is most comfortable at roughly 45–75 characters per line. Long measures cause line-return errors; short measures break rhythm. Applies regardless of style.
  • Hierarchy is created by contrast, not by ornament. Size, weight, spacing and position establish importance. Colour, borders and shadows are weaker instruments frequently used as substitutes for the stronger ones.
  • Whitespace is grouping. Proximity communicates relationship. Generous spacing is not luxury; it is the mechanism by which a reader understands what belongs with what.
  • The interface should defer to the content. Apple's Human Interface Guidelines have long stated three principles — clarity, deference and depth — with deference meaning the interface supports content and never competes with it, and depth meaning that layers and motion convey hierarchy. The deference principle, correctly understood, is an argument against most decorative effects, including several that appear in Apple's own marketing pages.
  • Motion should map to spatial or state relationships. Animation that shows where something came from or what changed aids comprehension. Animation applied because an element has entered the viewport does not.
  • Consistency reduces cognitive load. The same element should look and behave the same everywhere.
  • Accessibility constraints improve design for everyone. Contrast minimums, target sizes and focus visibility make interfaces better in bright sunlight, on cheap screens, and for tired people.

7.4 Current technical facts relevant to design decisions

  • Core Web Vitals. The three metrics remain Largest Contentful Paint (good below 2.5 seconds), Interaction to Next Paint (good below 200 milliseconds) and Cumulative Layout Shift (good below 0.1), each assessed at the 75th percentile of real field data over a rolling 28-day window. INP replaced First Input Delay in March 2024 and is the most commonly failed of the three; any guidance still referring to FID is out of date. The implication for design is direct: every animation library and scroll effect is an INP liability before it is anything else.
  • WCAG 2.2. Published October 2023, backward compatible with 2.1, adding nine success criteria and removing 4.1.1 Parsing. The two that most affect visual design here are 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), requiring pointer targets of at least 24×24 CSS pixels at Level AA with 44×44 recommended, and the focus-visibility criteria requiring that a focused component is not fully obscured by author content. These are constraints on the design, not tasks for the end of the build.
  • Font loading. font-display: swap combined with preloading and a metric-matched fallback eliminates the layout shift that otherwise makes a type-led design fail CLS — the precise risk of the direction recommended here.

7.5 Matters of taste, declared as such

The following are this document's preferences, defensible but not objectively correct. They are flagged so they can be overruled without breaking anything structural: a serif or humanist-sans display face rather than a geometric sans; a warm near-black rather than pure black; ruled section dividers rather than spacing alone; left-aligned rather than centred headings; and small-caps or letterspaced uppercase for metadata labels.

8. Visual Design Direction

8.1 The organising idea

Engineering documentation, set to editorial standards.

This is not a neutral choice. It is derived from three things already true about the author: he writes IEEE 830 specifications, UML sets and database design reports as a matter of habit; he has an articulated and unusual preference for serif, justified, structurally disciplined documents; and the strongest material in his record is written reasoning rather than visual product. A portfolio that looks like a well-made technical publication is therefore an honest expression of the work rather than a costume put on over it.

It also solves the practical problem. An early-career portfolio has no photography budget and no brand illustration. Directions that depend on imagery will fail. A direction that depends on typography, spacing and structure can be executed to a genuinely high standard with nothing but care.

And it is defensibly distinct from an Apple clone: it shares the underlying discipline — clarity, deference, hierarchy, restraint — while taking its surface vocabulary from technical publishing rather than from consumer product marketing.

8.2 The five governing rules

  1. Type does the work. Where a design problem could be solved with a graphic, a border, a shadow or a colour, try solving it with size, weight, spacing or position first.
  2. Every effect must name its job. If an element cannot be described as communicating hierarchy, state, progression or spatial relationship, it is removed. This applies with equal force to a shadow and to an animation.
  3. Space is structural. A rhythm of vertical spacing is designed first and adhered to; sections are separated by four to six times the space that separates paragraphs.
  4. Colour means something. A near-monochrome surface with exactly one functional accent, reserved for interaction and state. No decorative colour anywhere.
  5. Restraint is the differentiator. Given that generated portfolios are visually maximal by default, disciplined subtraction is now the clearest possible signal of a human designer.

8.3 Surface treatment

  • Rules over boxes. Separate content with 1px hairline rules and with space, not with bordered card containers. This alone removes most of the "AI-generated" signature.
  • Borderless images. Screenshots sit on the page with a single hairline rule and a caption, not inside a rounded, shadowed frame.
  • Radius is minimal and meaningful. 2px on interactive controls and code blocks; 0px on images, sections and containers.
  • Elevation is nearly absent. No box shadows in the resting state. A single subtle shadow is permitted on the mobile navigation overlay, where it genuinely signals a layer above the page.
  • No gradients on any surface, text or border.
  • Asymmetry by intent. A twelve-column grid used asymmetrically — text at columns 1–7, marginal metadata at 9–12 — produces visual interest structurally, without ornament.

8.5 Things this site will not have

Recorded explicitly so that they can be checked against during build and review: hero background gradients; glassmorphic panels; glowing or animated borders; cards that lift, tilt or scale on hover; bento grids; pill-shaped navigation; border radii above 4px; decorative blurred shapes; particle or mesh backgrounds; typewriter or scramble text effects; counters that animate up from zero; skill percentage bars; scroll-hijacked sections; cursor followers; unexplained emoji; and fade-up-on-scroll applied indiscriminately.

10. Interaction and Motion System

10.1 Motion principles

  1. Motion communicates one of four things: hierarchy (what is above what), state (what changed), progression (where I am) or spatial relationship (where this came from). Anything else is removed.
  2. Duration is proportional to distance travelled. 150ms for a colour change; 200–300ms for a small element; 400ms for a full-page transition. Nothing exceeds 400ms.
  3. Easing is directional: ease-out for entrances, ease-in for exits, ease-in-out for changes that begin and end on screen.
  4. Only transform and opacity are animated. Animating layout properties causes reflow and is a direct INP and CLS liability.
  5. Content is never gated behind motion. Every animated element is in its final, readable state in the server-rendered HTML; motion is applied on top and is entirely optional.

10.5 Keyboard interaction

  • Every interactive element reachable by Tab in a logical order matching visual order.
  • Visible focus on every focusable element: 2px accent ring at 2px offset, meeting the 3:1 contrast requirement against adjacent colours.
  • Escape closes the mobile navigation and returns focus to its trigger.
  • Skip-to-content as the first focusable element on every page.
  • Optional and low-cost: / to focus search if search is ever added; g then w or b as section jumps, documented in the footer. Implement only if they can be tested properly; an undiscoverable shortcut that intercepts typing is worse than no shortcut.

11. Accessibility Strategy

Target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA, verified rather than assumed. Accessibility is also, for this particular portfolio, a demonstration: a site built by a Software Engineer that passes AA cleanly is an argument that does not need to be made in words.

11.1 Semantic structure

  • One <h1> per page. No level skipped. Headings chosen for structure, never for size — size comes from the type scale.
  • Landmarks: <header>, <nav>, <main>, <article>, <aside>, <footer>. Multiple navigation landmarks distinguished by aria-label.
  • Native elements before ARIA in every case: <button> for actions, <a> for navigation, <details> for disclosure, <time> for dates, <table> with proper headers for tabular data.
  • Lists marked up as lists. Case study sections as <section> with an aria-labelledby pointing at their heading.

11.2 Contrast and colour

  • 4.5:1 minimum for body text, 3:1 for large text and non-text interface components — verified in both themes, with the computed ratios recorded in the token file as comments so regressions are visible in review.
  • No information carried by colour alone: active navigation uses a rule, form errors use icon and text, tags use rules and letterforms.
  • Tested at 200% zoom and under forced-colors mode.

11.3 Keyboard and focus

  • Full operability with no keyboard trap anywhere.
  • :focus-visible for the visible ring so mouse users are not shown rings unnecessarily, with a :focus fallback.
  • WCAG 2.2 2.4.11 satisfied: because the header never hides or overlays content on scroll, a focused element is never obscured by it. This is a case where a design decision made for other reasons also removes an accessibility risk.
  • Focus returned to the triggering element when the mobile navigation closes.

11.4 Target size — WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8

All pointer targets at least 24×24 CSS pixels, with 44×44 as the working standard for anything reached on a touch device. Inline prose links are exempt under the criterion, but list-item links, tags and navigation are not and are sized accordingly. Adjacent targets are spaced at least 8px apart.

12.2 Mobile as a designed experience

  • Full-bleed images. On mobile the image extends to the viewport edges while text keeps its padding. This produces a deliberate editorial rhythm rather than a narrowed desktop page.
  • No hover dependencies anywhere. Every hover-revealed thing is either always visible on touch or replaced by an explicit control. Verified by auditing the site with hover disabled.
  • Touch targets. 44px minimum with 8px separation.
  • Thumb reach. Primary actions in the lower two-thirds. The mobile menu button is bottom-right-reachable in the open panel, not only top-right.

  • Mobile-first CSS, with min-width media queries.

  • Tested on a real low-to-mid-range Android device, not only in a desktop browser's device emulator. Given the intended audience and the author's own context, this is not optional.

Alternative: next-mdx-remote with a hand-written front-matter loader, which is more work but has no framework dependency at all.
Syntax highlightingShiki, at build timeBuild-time highlighting ships zero JavaScript to the client. This matters: a client-side highlighter is often the largest script on a technical blog, and it is a direct INP cost on the pages that most need to be fast.
AnimationCSS transitions plus the View Transitions API. Motion (formerly Framer Motion) only if needed.Given the motion inventory in Deliverable 10, almost everything is a CSS transition. 13.1.1 Technologies deliberately excluded

  • A headless CMS (Sanity, Contentful, Payload). Solves a collaboration problem that does not exist for a single author, and introduces a runtime dependency, an API key and a monthly quota.
  • A database. Nothing on the site requires persistent state. View counters and comments are not worth a database, an origin region and an operational surface.
  • Three.js or WebGL. No content requires it; it would contradict the visual direction and cost hundreds of kilobytes.
  • GSAP with ScrollTrigger. Its principal use case here would be the scroll-triggered animation the design explicitly rejects.
  • A component library (shadcn/ui, MUI, Chakra). The component set is small and specific; a library would fight the design system rather than serve it. The author already prefers custom components.
  • Astro. Genuinely excellent for exactly this kind of site and would ship less JavaScript. Not recommended here because Next.js is already familiar, and the fluency gained is directly reusable on the music platform and in employment. This is a deliberate trade of a marginal technical advantage for a compounding practical one — and it is stated rather than hidden.

  • Self-hosted variable woff2, subset to Latin plus the punctuation actually used.
  • font-display: swap with next/font/local, which generates a metric-matched fallback so the swap does not move the layout.
  • Preload only the two faces used above the fold — display serif and body sans. The mono face loads normally, since no code appears in any first viewport.
  • Variable fonts mean one file per family rather than four static weights: fewer requests and fewer bytes for more typographic range.

15.4 JavaScript

  • Server Components by default. Six client components in total, named in Section 13.3.
  • Syntax highlighting at build time — zero client cost.
  • No animation library unless a specific interaction proves it necessary.
  • Dynamic import with next/dynamic for anything below the fold, such as interactive diagrams.
  • @next/bundle-analyzer run before each release; any dependency above 10KB must be justified in the pull request description.

15.5 Delivery and caching

  • Fully static output on an edge network. Immutable hashed asset filenames with a one-year cache lifetime; HTML revalidated.
  • next/link prefetching on viewport entry for internal routes — a real advantage of client-side routing, and the reason navigation between case studies feels instant.
  • Third-party requests: zero, apart from the analytics beacon. No external fonts, no icon CDN, no embedded widgets.

15.6 Progressive enhancement

A working baseline without JavaScript, verified by testing with scripts disabled: all content readable; all navigation functional; the contact form submits via a Server Action; disclosure elements open and close natively; the theme falls back to the operating system preference. What is lost: the theme toggle, the reading progress bar, the copy button, view transitions and the hover preview. Every one of those is an enhancement, and none of them carries content.

15.7 Monitoring

  • Lighthouse CI on every pull request with the budgets above enforced as hard failures.
  • Real-user vitals collected through the analytics provider.
  • A quarterly manual check on an actual mid-range device over mobile data.

17.6 The design direction

Engineering documentation, set to editorial standards.

The reference point for quality is Apple's digital design work — clean layouts, strong typography, generous whitespace, restrained effects, excellent hierarchy, purposeful animation, content foregrounded above everything. Do not produce an Apple clone. Take the underlying discipline, not the surface vocabulary. Specifically: adopt one idea per viewport, typography as primary imagery, extreme spacing discipline, and a severely restricted palette. Do not adopt full-bleed cinematic photography or translucent glass materials.

The surface vocabulary should come instead from technical publishing and editorial design: serif display type, hairline rules, captioned figures, marginal metadata, asymmetric grids, and a structural rather than decorative use of space. The subject writes formal technical specifications as a matter of habit and has strong typographic taste, so this direction is an honest expression of him rather than a costume.

Five governing rules

  1. Type does the work. Before reaching for a graphic, a border, a shadow or a colour, try solving the problem with size, weight, spacing or position.
  2. Every effect must name its job. If an element cannot be described as communicating hierarchy, state, progression or spatial relationship, remove it.
  3. Space is structural. Separation between sections should be four to six times the separation within them.
  4. Colour means something. Near-monochrome, with one functional accent reserved for links, focus and active state.
  5. Restraint is the differentiator. Disciplined subtraction is the clearest signal of human design.

17.13 Accessibility expectations

Design to WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Specifically: 4.5:1 contrast for body text and 3:1 for large text and interface components, verified in both themes; a visible 2px focus ring at 2px offset on every interactive element, shown in the mockups; pointer targets of at least 24×24 CSS pixels, with 44×44 on touch; no information carried by colour alone; a skip-to-content link as the first focusable element; visible form labels with errors shown in text and shape as well as colour; a logical heading hierarchy with no level skipped. Include a focus-state mockup for the navigation, a button and a form field.

17.14 Responsive expectations

Deliver every page at four widths: 1440px, 1024px, 768px and 375px. Layouts must be re-composed, not shrunk. Required transformations:

  • Header collapses to wordmark, theme toggle and menu button; the menu opens a full-screen panel with generously spaced targets — not a cramped dropdown.
  • Work entries lose the hover preview entirely and show the image inline instead.
  • Images go full-bleed to the viewport edges on mobile while text keeps its padding.
  • The case study's marginal table of contents becomes a collapsed disclosure at the top.
  • Tables of more than three columns become definition lists.
  • Code blocks go full-bleed with horizontal scroll inside their own container, so the page body never scrolls sideways.
  • The alternating timeline becomes a single column with a left rule.
  • Display type reduces to 40px but keeps its designed line breaks.
  • Body text stays at 17px.

17.15 Prohibited — do not produce generic AI or "vibecoded" UI

  • Bento grids.
  • Border radii above 4px on any container.
